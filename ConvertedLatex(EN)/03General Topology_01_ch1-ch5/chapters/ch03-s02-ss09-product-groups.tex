\subsection{PRODUCTS OF TOPOLOGICAL GROUPS}

Let \((\mathbf{G}_i)_{i\in I}\) be a family of topological groups. Then we can define a group structure on the product set

\[
\mathbf {G} = \prod_ {i \in I} \mathbf {G} _ {i}
\]

(the product of the group structures of the \(\mathbf{G}_i\) ) by defining \((x_i) \cdot (y_i) = (x_i y_i)\) . If \(e_i\) is the identity element of \(\mathbf{G}_i\) , then \(e = (e_i)\) is the identity element of \(\mathbf{G}\) , and we have \((x_i)^{-1} = (x_i^{-1})\) . The product topology (Chapter I, § 2, no. 3) of the topologies of the \(\mathbf{G}_i\) is compatible with this group structure. For the mapping \(((x_i), (y_i)) \to (x_i y_i^{-1})\) of \(\mathbf{G} \times \mathbf{G}\) into \(\mathbf{G}\) is the composition of the mapping \(((x_i, y_i)) \to (x_i y_i^{-1})\) of

\[
\prod_ {i \in I} G _ {i} \times G _ {i} \text {   into   } G,
\]

and the canonical mapping \(((x_{i}), (y_{i})) \to ((x_{i}, y_{i}))\) of

\[
\mathbf {G} \times \mathbf {G} \quad \text { onto } \quad \prod_ {i \in \mathbf {I}} (\mathbf {G} _ {i} \times \mathbf {G} _ {i});
\]

and both these mappings are continuous (Chapter I, § 4, no. 1, Corollary 1 of Proposition 1, and Proposition 2).

DEFINITION 2. The topological group obtained by giving the product set

\[
\mathbf {G} = \prod_ {i \in \mathbf {I}} \mathbf {G} _ {i}
\]

the group structure which is the product of the group structures of the \(G_{i}\) and the topology which is the product of the topologies of the \(G_{i}\) is called the product of the topological groups \(G_{i}\) .

If \((\mathbf{J}_{\kappa})_{\kappa \in \mathbb{K}}\) is a partition of I, then G is isomorphic to the product of the topological groups \(\prod_{i \in J_{\kappa}} G_{i}\) (associativity of the product).

If \(\mathbf{H}_{i}\) is a subgroup of \(\mathbf{G}_{i}\) , then the product of the topological groups \(\mathbf{H}_{i}\) is isomorphic to the subgroup \(\prod_{i} \mathbf{H}_{i}\) of \(\prod_{i} \mathbf{G}_{i}\) . In particular, if \(J\) is any subset of \(I\) , and \(J' = C_{J}\) , then the topological group \(\prod_{i \in J} \mathbf{G}_{i}\) is isomorphic to the normal subgroup \(\mathbf{G}_{J}' = (\prod_{i \in J} \mathbf{G}_{i}) \times (\prod_{i' \notin J} \{e_{i'}\})\) of \(\mathbf{G}\) . Since the projection of every open set is an open set, the projection \(\mathbf{pr}_{J}\) of \(\mathbf{G}\) onto \(\prod_{i \in J} \mathbf{G}_{i}\) is a strict morphism, and consequently the quotient group \(\mathbf{G} / \mathbf{G}_{J}'\) is isomorphic to \(\mathbf{G}_{J'}'\) ; \(\mathbf{G}\) is isomorphic to the product \(\mathbf{G}_{J}' \times (\mathbf{G} / \mathbf{G}_{J}')\) .

PROPOSITION 25. Let \((\mathbf{G}_{i})_{i\in I}\) be a family of topological groups, and let H be the normal subgroup of \(\mathbf{G} = \prod_{i\in I}\mathbf{G}_i\) consisting of all \(x = (x_{i})\) such that the \(x_{i}\) are equal to the identity element \(e_i\) of \(\mathbf{G}_i\) except for a finite number of indices. Then the subgroup H is dense in G.

This is a particular case of Chapter I, § 4, no. 3, Proposition 8.

Let \((\mathbf{X}_i)_{i \in I}\) be a family of topological spaces, and for each \(i \in I\) let \(\mathbf{G}_i\) be a topological group operating continuously on \(\mathbf{X}_i\) . It is clear that the product group \(\mathbf{G} = \prod_{i \in I} \mathbf{G}_i\) then operates continuously on the product space \(\mathbf{X} = \prod_{i \in I} \mathbf{X}_i\) according to the law

\[
\left(\left(s _ {i}\right), \left(x _ {i}\right)\right)\rightarrow \left(s _ {i}. x _ {i}\right)
\]

(Chapter I, § 4, no. 1, Proposition 1, Corollary 1, and Proposition 2). Moreover the orbit under G of a point \(x = (x_{i})\) of X is the product of the orbits of the \(x_{i}\) (with respect to the groups \(G_{i}\) ). Let \(\varphi_{i}\) be the canonical mapping of \(X_{i}\) onto \(X_{i}/G_{i}\) , and let \(\varphi = (\varphi_{i})\) be the product mapping of X onto \(\prod_{i\in I}(X_{i}/G_{i})\) ; then the preceding remark shows that the bijection canonically associated with \(\varphi\) maps the orbit space X/G onto \(\prod_{i\in I}(X_{i}/G_{i})\) . Moreover:

PROPOSITION 26. The bijection of X/G onto \(\prod_{i\in I}(\mathbf{X}_i / \mathbf{G}_i)\) canonically associated with \((\varphi_i)\) is a homeomorphism.

For since the \(\varphi_{i}\) are surjective and open, it follows that \(\varphi = (\varphi_{i})\) is an open mapping (Chapter I, § 5, no. 3, Corollary to Proposition 8).

COROLLARY. Let \((\mathbf{G}_{i})_{i\in I}\) be a family of topological groups, and for each \(i\in I\) let \(\mathbf{H}_i\) be a normal subgroup of \(\mathbf{G}_i\) ; let \(\varphi_i\) denote the canonical mapping of \(\mathbf{G}_i\) onto \(\mathbf{G}_i / \mathbf{H}_i\) . Let \(\mathbf{G} = \prod_{i\in I}\mathbf{G}_i\) , \(\mathbf{H} = \prod_{i\in I}\mathbf{H}_i\) . Then the bijective homomorphism of \(\mathbf{G} / \mathbf{H}\) onto \(\prod_{i\in I}(\mathbf{G}_i / \mathbf{H}_i)\) associated with the continuous homomorphism \((x_i)\to (\varphi_i(x_i))\) is an isomorphism of topological groups.

For this homomorphism is an isomorphism of group structures.

Remark. If G is a commutative topological group, written additively, then the mapping \((x,y)\to x+y\) of \(G\times G\) onto G is a strict morphism. For since \((x+x')+(y+y')=(x+y)+(x'+y')\) it is a homomorphism of \(G\times G\) onto G; also it is continuous, and the image of a neighbourhood \(V\times V\) of the origin in \(G\times G\) under this mapping is the neighbourhood \(V+V\) of the origin in G.
