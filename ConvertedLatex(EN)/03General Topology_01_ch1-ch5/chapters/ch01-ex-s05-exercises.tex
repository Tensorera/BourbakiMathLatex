\item
  Show that an equivalence relation R on a topological space X is closed if and only if, for each subset A of X, the saturation of the closure of A contains the closure of the saturation of A. Give an example where R is closed and these two sets can be distinct.
\end{enumerate}

* 2) Show that the equivalence relation \(S: x \equiv y \pmod{1}\) on \(R\) is not closed. If \(A = [0,1]\) show that the induced relation \(S_A\) on \(A\) is closed but not open. *

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi})}
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\tightlist
\item
  Let \(\Gamma\) be a finite group of homeomorphisms of a topological space \(X\) , and let \(R\) be the equivalence relation: ``there exists \(\sigma \in \Gamma\) such that \(y = \sigma(x)\)''. Show that the relation \(R\) is both open and closed.
\end{enumerate}

* 4) Let \(\Gamma\) be the group of homeomorphisms of the quotient space \(\mathbf{T}\) of \(\mathbf{R}\) consisting of the identity and the homeomorphism induced on \(\mathbf{T}\) by the homeomorphism \(x \to 1/2 + x\) of \(\mathbf{R}\) onto itself. Let \(\mathbf{S}\) be the equivalence relation ``there exists \(\sigma \in \Gamma\) such that \(y = \sigma(x)\) '' on \(\mathbf{T}\) ; \(\mathbf{S}\) is both open and closed (Exercise 3). Show that \(\mathbf{T}\) has no continuous section with respect to \(\mathbf{S}\) , although both \(\mathbf{T}\) and \(\mathbf{T}/\mathbf{S}\) are compact, connected and locally connected. Let \(\mathbf{A}\) be the canonical image in \(\mathbf{T}\) of the interval \([0,1/2]\) of \(\mathbf{R}\) . Show that \(\mathbf{A}\) is locally closed in \(\mathbf{T}\) but that the quotient space \(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{A}}\) is not homeomorphic to the canonical image of \(\mathbf{A}\) in \(\mathbf{T}/\mathbf{S}\) . *

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi})}
\setcounter{enumi}{4}
\tightlist
\item
  Let X be a topological space and let R, S be two equivalence relations on X such that R implies S.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\alph{enumi})}
\tightlist
\item
  Show that if S is open (resp. closed) then S/R is an open (resp. closed) equivalence relation on X/R; show also that the converse is not necessarily true. Show that S can be open (resp. closed) but not R.
\item
  Suppose that R is open (resp. closed). Show that S/R is open (resp. closed) if and only if S is open (resp. closed).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi})}
\setcounter{enumi}{5}
\item
  Let S be the equivalence relation on the rational line Q which is obtained by identifying all the points of Z. Show that S is closed, and that if U is the relation of equality on Q, then the canonical bijection of \((\mathbf{Q} \times \mathbf{Q}) / (\mathbf{U} \times \mathbf{S})\) onto \(\mathbf{Q} \times (\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{S})\) is not a homeomorphism.
\item
  Let X and Y be two non-empty topological spaces and \(f: X \to Y\) a surjective map. Show that f is closed if and only if the mapping \(y \to (\overline{f} y)\) of Y into \(\mathfrak{P}_{0}(X)\) is continuous when \(\mathfrak{P}_{0}(X)\) carries the topology \(C_{\Omega}\) (resp. \(C_{\Phi}\) ) (§ 2, Exercise 7).
\end{enumerate}

Deduce that the topology induced on a quotient set X/R of a non-empty topological space X by the topology \(\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}\) (resp. \(\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}\) ) coincides with the quotient of the topology of X by R if and only if R is closed (resp. open) (cf.~§ 3, Exercise 16).
